# Stock research

Use this procedure when the user requests research on a company or stock. Follow the
user's specific question and requested depth; use the full outline for a general report.

## Identify the subject

- Confirm the company name, ticker, exchange, and trading currency from public sources.
- Ask for clarification when the requested name or ticker is ambiguous.
- Use runtime.current_time to establish the research date.

## Gather information

- Use the available hosted web search tool for current research. Prioritize company
  filings, earnings releases, and investor relations pages. Use reputable reporting
  for additional context and link material claims to supporting sources.
- Start with the most relevant sources and work within the available search budget.
  If time, tool availability, or search limits prevent verification, say what remains
  unchecked. Do not present remembered information as freshly researched.
- Record the financial period for results and the publication/event dates for news.
  Distinguish reported facts, management guidance, and your interpretation.
- Do not invent missing metrics, explanations for price movements, or citations.
  Include a stock price only when requested or relevant, with its source, timestamp,
  currency, and any known delay. Web search is not a guaranteed live quote feed.

## Optional portfolio context

- Only consult portfolio tools when the user asks about their holdings, exposure,
  or position performance. Public company research does not require ownership.
- Use the available portfolio status and holdings tools, then prices or performance
  tools as needed. Keep the saved IBKR report date separate from the research date.
- Portfolio prices are report-date closes for reported holdings. Do not describe
  them as live prices or use them as quotes for stocks outside the saved holdings.

## Default report

Keep the initial report concise; expand when requested.

1. Identity and research date: company, ticker, exchange, and currency.
2. Business: what the company sells and how it earns revenue.
3. Latest results: relevant reported metrics, period, and comparisons if verified.
4. Recent developments: major dated announcements and their possible implications.
5. Risks and open questions: concrete uncertainties and missing information.
6. Portfolio context: include only when requested and available.

Place source links beside the claims they support. Explain conflicting sources or
unverified details. End with a short synthesis tied to the user's question, separating
facts from interpretation. Do not assume the user wants a buy/sell recommendation.
